    <h2 id="introduction">Introduction</h2>
    <p>The balance of power between us and the corporations we talk through keeps tilting their way. Sure, technology has handed users some power. But let's be honest, we haven't really won anything big against the corporations yet. Here's why I think users have always been on the back foot online, and probably will be for a while.</p>
    <div class="post-section-head" aria-hidden="true">
      <span class="head-tab"><i class="icon-scale"></i><span>02</span></span>
    </div>
    <h2 id="asymmetry-of-resources-and-expertise">Asymmetry of Resources and Expertise</h2>
    <p>Corporations have money and expertise you and I just don't. They can hire whole teams of specialists: data people, marketers, UX folks. They can pour money into the latest tech, AI, and algorithms to hoover up your data and crunch it. You, on your own, don't have the cash or the technical firepower to fight that on level terms.</p>
    <div class="post-section-head" aria-hidden="true">
      <span class="head-tab"><i class="icon-file-text"></i><span>03</span></span>
    </div>
    <h2 id="terms-of-service-and-legal-agreements">Terms of Service and Legal Agreements</h2>
    <p>The terms of service are written for them, not for you. They're long, dense, and stuffed with legalese most people never read. And you get an &ldquo;all-or-nothing&rdquo; deal: accept everything, or don't use the thing. No room to negotiate, no picking the bits you're fine with. That's not a fair fight.</p>
    <div class="post-pull-quote">
      <aside>
        <p class="pq-quote">&ldquo;You get an all-or-nothing deal: accept everything, or don't use the thing at all.&rdquo;</p>
      </aside>
    </div>
    <div class="post-section-head" aria-hidden="true">
      <span class="head-tab"><i class="icon-eye"></i><span>04</span></span>
    </div>
    <h2 id="privacy-concerns-and-data-exploitation">Privacy Concerns and Data Exploitation</h2>
    <p>Privacy has quietly been eaten away. Corporations collect and pick apart huge amounts of your data to run their business, aim ads at you, and work out how you behave. People say they care about privacy, but the companies mostly control the story and decide how much of it you actually get. Every now and then there's an outcry or a new regulation, and somehow they find a way around it. You're left with very little say over your own information.</p>
    <div class="post-section-head" aria-hidden="true">
      <span class="head-tab"><i class="icon-lock"></i><span>05</span></span>
    </div>
    <h2 id="technological-lock-in-and-dependency">Technological Lock-In and Dependency</h2>
    <p>Then there's lock-in. You get pulled into one company's ecosystem and slowly come to depend on it, because everything is convenient, everything connects, and everyone else is already there. Once you're deep in, leaving is a pain: the time, the effort, the data you'd lose. That stickiness keeps them in charge and makes any user-led revolt pretty unlikely.</p>
    <div class="post-section-head" aria-hidden="true">
      <span class="head-tab"><i class="icon-landmark"></i><span>06</span></span>
    </div>
    <h2 id="lobbying-power-and-influence">Lobbying Power and Influence</h2>
    <p>And they've got the ear of the people who make the rules. Lobbying and political donations let them bend regulations in their own favour, protecting their patch and keeping users boxed in. Even when someone tries to rein them in, users just don't have the same clout to push real change through.</p>
    <div class="post-callout is-note">
      <aside>

        <p class="callout-body">Tilting this back our way takes a few things at once: people acting together, regulators actually paying attention, and new models built to put users first. Nothing shifts until we name the problem honestly and start chipping away at it.</p>
      </aside>
    </div>
    <div class="post-section-head" aria-hidden="true">
      <span class="head-tab"><i class="icon-flag"></i><span>07</span></span>
    </div>
    <h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2>
    <p>We've made a bit of progress, mostly in waking people up to privacy and pushing for transparency. But actually winning against the corporations? Still out of reach. The gaps in money, contracts, data, dependency, and political pull all keep users on the losing side. The way forward is that same short list: act together, get regulators to actually watch, and build alternatives that put users first. It only starts once we're honest about how lopsided the whole thing is.</p>
