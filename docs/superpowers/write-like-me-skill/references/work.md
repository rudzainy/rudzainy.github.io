# Work voice (case studies / portfolio)

## Fingerprint
- **Tone:** Quietly proud but plain-spoken. He documents what he built and why, like a maker walking you through the work, not a marketer selling it. Fond of the *symbolism* behind a design choice, and honest about the boring facts (rejected drafts, discontinued sites, taken-down domains).
- **Rhythm:** Mostly short-to-medium declaratives, one idea per sentence. Opens plain, then layers detail. The old `.md` pieces are terse and factual; the Hoojah case study runs longer and more reflective. Both build fact by fact rather than in one big pitch.
- **Vocabulary:** Real practitioner words: UI/UX, HTML & CSS, responsive design, information architecture, sitemap, Joomla, CMS, LAMP/WAMP, deployment, analytics. Design-crit verbs: *encapsulates, signifies, underscoring, illustrate*. Names the exact stack and year. Malay shows up mostly as proper nouns and place names (Persatuan Maritim Malaysia, Kuala Lumpur, `hoojah.my`), not heavy code-switch, this category runs more English than Life/Balance.
- **Structure:** Opens with a one-line setup (who asked, what it was, when). Older pieces sometimes lead with a meta table (Project / Responsibilities / Technology Stack / Year / Remark) and captioned images. Then walks the work: for logos, decodes each symbol element-by-element; for builds, goes background then design then feedback/takeaways. Closes on a plain status note (discontinued, taken down, "remains alive without recent updates") or a lesson carried forward.
- **Signature quirks:** Third-person distancing when looking back at his younger self ("a younger me", "He was just starting out"). "however" mid-sentence instead of "but". Slightly formal, almost literary logo copy (North Star, guiding light). Leaves in honest imperfections and doesn't over-polish the older notes. Emoji essentially absent from Work prose.

## Verbatim calibration quotes
> This design was rejected, however I have always liked the symbolism behind the combination of asterisk (*) and the letter A.
> In 2008, a younger me joined a local web development company as a web designer.
> The logo encapsulates various maritime symbols, underscoring its seafaring essence.
> It's a pet project that I started working on in 2013.

## Do
- Open with a plain, concrete setup: who asked, what it was, the year, the stack. Facts first.
- For logos/brand work, decode the symbolism piece by piece (this element signifies X, that shape means Y).
- Name the real tools and constraints (Joomla, LAMP, the client, the template you built on top of).
- State project status honestly, even when unglamorous: rejected, discontinued, domain taken down.
- Keep first-person and reflective on lessons learned, but earn it with the actual work behind it.

## Don't
- Don't slip into marketing gloss or hype ("masterclass", "north star", "game-changing", "revolutionary"). He describes, he doesn't sell.
- Don't flatten into STE / controlled-language bullet soup, keep the plain narrative voice and asides.
- Don't invent metrics, praise, or outcomes that aren't in the record.
- Don't force Malay in, this category is naturally English-heavier; only the real proper nouns are Malay.
- Don't use the em-dash character. Use commas, periods, colons, or parentheses instead.
