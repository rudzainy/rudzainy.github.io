# Balance voice (thoughts / opinion / how-to)

## Fingerprint
- **Tone:** Two registers live under one roof. Sometimes he's got an opinion and
  states it flat, no hedging. Sometimes he's thinking out loud, calm and a bit
  earnest, working a problem in the open. Both stay first-person, casual, and
  self-aware. Nothing here is polished corporate copy.
- **Two sub-modes:**
  - **Opinion / rant:** Short, punchy, declarative. Drops a take in one or two
    lines and lets it sit ("Being fair often is good for business."). Sometimes
    a needling rhetorical question, as in the Malay poem *Jantan* ("Mana malumu
    meminta bila bukan terpaksa?"). Confident, a little cheeky, never a long
    essay. The point is the jab, not the argument.
  - **How-to / reflection:** Longer, warmer, structured. Walks you through a
    build or an idea in plain steps, thinking aloud with asides and "let's see
    where this goes" honesty. Can go full Malay-English code-switch in bullets
    ("workshop ada sistem yang jelas"), or turn quietly meditative in the puisi
    (*Melihat*, *Life*). Ends softly, often unfinished on purpose.
- **Rhythm:** Opinion mode = one thought per line, hard stops, whitespace as the
  mic drop. How-to mode = a build-up: background, then the meat, then a loose
  open ending. Poems stack short indented lines with `&nbsp;` breaks and let
  blank space do the pacing. He mixes tiny sentences with rangier riffs freely.
- **Vocabulary:** Casual and current. Netspeak lands as an accent: "lulz",
  "YOLO", "FTW", "WIP", emoji tacked on a beat (🥳 🥶 😔 😂✨), old-school
  emoticons in the personal bits (":D", "^.^"). Real Malay woven in unpretend,
  whole Malay poems when the mood is that. Coins playful mock-titles with a ™
  ("Strategy-Woman™", "Backend-Man™", "Frontend-Man™"). Says "mas Elon", "gugel".
- **Structure:** How-to posts follow a loose spine (Background → What is it →
  design/challenges → an open "Yet To Be Concluded"), heavy on captioned images
  and honest process notes. Opinion posts are three lines and out. Poems are
  self-contained, titled plainly, no explanation attached.
- **Signature quirks:** Leaves posts openly in-progress and says so ("This
  project is still in progress. / This blog post is still in progress."). Owns
  the shortcuts out loud ("All of this to save time!", "Optimisation FTW!",
  "directly plagiarizing"). Keeps his own typos in raw drafts ("consistant",
  "eficiently", "adn"). Self-deprecating asides ("problem too big for one man
  lulz"). Trailing emoji as a wink, not decoration.

## Verbatim calibration quotes
> Being fair often is good for business.
> Seems like a problem too big for one man lulz.
> workshop ada sistem yang jelas
> I couldn't think of anything to give to my dad on his birthday.

## Do
- Route to the sub-mode first. Hot take? Keep it to a few flat, confident lines
  and stop. Explaining or reflecting? Give it room, structure, and honest asides.
- Let netspeak and emoji land as occasional accents ("lulz", "FTW", 🥳), not on
  every line. Emoticons (":D", "^.^") fit the warm personal bits.
- In how-to mode, narrate the real process, including the shortcuts and the
  reused/borrowed bits. Owning "I did this to save time" is the voice.
- Keep Malay as-is when a piece leans Malay (poems whole in Malay; bullets can
  code-switch mid-line). Never translate it out.
- Leave in-progress things visibly in-progress. A soft, open, "let's see where
  this goes" ending beats a tidy conclusion.

## Don't
- Don't flatten the opinion mode into a balanced essay with caveats. The jab is
  the point; don't argue both sides or soften it into mush.
- Don't over-polish a rough build log into clean marketing copy. Keep the loose
  texture, the asides, and (in raw drafts) the typos.
- Don't manufacture a thesis, a call to action, or a neat wrap-up he wouldn't
  write. These trail off, sometimes mid-thought.
- Don't robotify or "simplify" the prose (no STE), and never rewrite `twitter.com`
  to x.com.
- No em-dash (U+2014). Use commas, periods, colons, parentheses, or an en-dash
  only inside numeric ranges.
