    <h2>The brief</h2>
    <p class="lead">Kakiseni is an arts and events platform in Southeast Asia. The brief wasn't "make the homepage prettier." It was narrower and harder than that: get more venues and organisers to actually submit their listings. So the homepage has one real job. It has to sell submitting, not just browsing.</p>
    <p>I set a few constraints before touching a layout. Mobile-first, because that's how most people arrive. Seven arts disciplines, each colour-coded so you can read the mix at a glance. A submission funnel that earns its place on the homepage instead of hiding on some /submit page: benefits, reach numbers, a couple of testimonials. And a pricing ladder kept deliberately plain, three rungs: Free Listing, Featured Listing around RM3,000, Partnership around RM10,000. For the scaffolding I reached for a Batik design system I already had lying around. That was a speed decision, not a design statement. It let me prototype fast and argue about the real thing: the layout.</p>

    <h2>Three directions</h2>
    <p>Then I drew three homepage directions, each one across mobile, tablet and desktop.</p>
    <h3 style="margin:var(--space-5) 0 8px;font-family:var(--font-display);text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:var(--tracking-display);font-weight:400;color:var(--cat-ink)"><i class="icon-calendar me-2" aria-hidden="true"></i>1a · Weekend first</h3>
    <div class="post-figure">
      <figure>
        <span class="smart-image is-zoom"><img src="/images/portfolio/kakiseni/1a-weekend-first.png" alt="Direction 1a: listings lead, colour dialled back, featured shows as poster cards" loading="lazy" data-fallback="1a Weekend first"></span>
        <figcaption><i class="icon-image me-1" aria-hidden="true"></i>1a, Weekend first: listings lead, colour dialled back.</figcaption>
      </figure>
    </div>
    <p>The listings lead. Colour dialled right back, featured shows sitting inline as big poster cards, the organiser pitch tucked into a sidebar on desktop. Built for the person who showed up looking for something to do this weekend. Serve them first, sell to them second.</p>
    <h3 style="margin:var(--space-5) 0 8px;font-family:var(--font-display);text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:var(--tracking-display);font-weight:400;color:var(--cat-ink)"><i class="icon-image me-2" aria-hidden="true"></i>1b · The cover</h3>
    <div class="post-figure">
      <figure>
        <span class="smart-image is-zoom"><img src="/images/portfolio/kakiseni/1b-the-cover.png" alt="Direction 1b: Miriam Omar artwork full-bleed, seven disciplines as blocks, editorial rows" loading="lazy" data-fallback="1b The cover"></span>
        <figcaption><i class="icon-image me-1" aria-hidden="true"></i>1b, The cover: artwork full-bleed, disciplines as blocks.</figcaption>
      </figure>
    </div>
    <p>Miriam Omar's artwork runs full-bleed across the top, like a magazine cover. The seven disciplines line up as blocks underneath, listings fall into three-up editorial rows, and the desktop hero splits into two columns. This one leads with mood before it leads with function.</p>
    <h3 style="margin:var(--space-5) 0 8px;font-family:var(--font-display);text-transform:uppercase;letter-spacing:var(--tracking-display);font-weight:400;color:var(--cat-ink)"><i class="icon-door-open me-2" aria-hidden="true"></i>1c · Two doors</h3>
    <div class="post-figure">
      <figure>
        <span class="smart-image is-zoom"><img src="/images/portfolio/kakiseni/1c-two-doors.png" alt="Direction 1c: split screen for audiences and organisers, reach strip, three pricing tiers" loading="lazy" data-fallback="1c Two doors"></span>
        <figcaption><i class="icon-image me-1" aria-hidden="true"></i>1c, Two doors: audiences and organisers, split down the middle.</figcaption>
      </figure>
    </div>
    <p>The homepage splits down the middle: audiences on one side, organisers on the other. A reach strip between them carrying the numbers, and the three pricing tiers laid out three-across. It's the most literal answer to the brief. Here's your door, here's your door.</p>
    <p>I'm not going to tell you which one wins. I genuinely haven't decided, and that's the honest part. Each one solves the same problem from a different angle: one leads with the listings, one with the art, one with the choice. So I'll leave it with you. Which door would you walk through?</p>
