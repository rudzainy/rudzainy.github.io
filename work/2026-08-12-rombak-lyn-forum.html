Rombak LYN Forum

An unsolicited redesign of Malaysia's biggest forum. 81 million posts and 944,000 members live on an interface built for a 1024×768 monitor, and this is my attempt at moving the furniture without chasing the regulars out of the house.

The one-line thesis

The forum's problem is not that it looks old. It's that everything is equally loud, so the smartest thread and the dumbest one arrive at your eyes with identical weight.

Role

Everything: research, IA, UI

Method

Heuristic audit, IA restructure, hi-fi screens

Scope

7 screens, mobile + desktop, one moderation system

Timebox

A few weekends

Disclaimer

Not affiliated with Lowyat.NET. No original screens are reproduced here, every pixel below is my own proposal. 🤙

01 · nobody asked me

Summary

I've been reading this forum since I was a teenager looking up motherboard prices. It is still, in 2026, where you go when you want a Malaysian who actually knows the answer, about a warranty, a landlord, a broken Astro box, a used lens. That accumulated knowledge is the asset. The interface is the tax you pay to reach it.

So I gave myself three constraints: fix the mobile experience, fix navigation and density, and make moderation and thread quality visible, without turning the place into a soft, airy social app. The regulars didn't stay 20 years because it was pretty. They stayed because it is fast, dense, and honest.

The result: nine top-level categories collapse into three lanes, the thread list starts carrying signal instead of counters, and every moderation action becomes something you can see and appeal. Density stays high, it just gets ranked.

02 · 81 million posts

Background

A national archive that looks like a mailing list

The board runs the classic 2000s forum pattern: category → subforum → paginated thread list → paginated posts. It has survived Facebook groups, WhatsApp, Reddit and TikTok, which tells you the content is worth the friction. But the friction is real, and it lands hardest on phones, where most of the traffic now is.

81M+

posts on the board, two decades of answers nobody has indexed properly

944K

registered members, a large share of them dormant lurkers who still read daily

9

top-level categories, over a hundred subforums beneath them

3

taps minimum to reach a thread from the index, five if you want the newest post

Who I'm designing for

Loyal to a fault. High trust in the platform itself, people still meet strangers in a mall car park because a forum profile said the seller was okay. And genuinely clever: engineers, mechanics, accountants, doctors, all answering for free. Any redesign that treats them like new users of a social app, confirmed kena reject terus.

03 · two weeks of notes

What's Actually Broken

I ran a plain heuristic pass on my own phone over two weeks of normal reading. No lab, no participants, just notes every time I got annoyed. Three clusters came out of it, and they map exactly onto what I set out to fix.

Problem 01 · Mobile

A desktop table squeezed onto a 390px screen. Pagination controls smaller than a fingertip. Reply is a round trip to another page. Reading a 400-page thread means guessing which page number you left off at.

Cost: the phone majority reads but rarely posts.

Problem 02 · Density & nav

Density itself is fine, the ranking is missing. Every row shows the same four counters, none of which tell you whether the thread is worth opening. Getting between two subforums means going back to the top of the tree.

Cost: people camp in one subforum and never see the rest.

Problem 03 · Quality & moderation

Moderation is invisible until it lands on you. Rules live in a sticky post nobody reads, removals appear as gaps, and a thread's health is something you infer from the tone of the last three pages.

Cost: mods look arbitrary, and good threads rot unnoticed.

04 · so i can say no

Four Rules I Gave Myself

1

Rank density, don't remove it. A screen may stay full. It may not stay flat.

2

Every number must change a decision. If a counter doesn't help you decide whether to tap, it goes.

3

Moderation in the open. Removals, locks and warnings are shown, explained, and appealable, in place.

4

Reward the veterans, don't gate the newcomers. Twenty years of post history should buy visible standing, not private privilege.

05 · nothing deleted

IA: Nine Categories Become Three Lanes

The nine top-level categories are a filing cabinet, not a mental model. In practice people come for one of three reasons: to solve something technical, to talk to Malaysians, or to buy and sell. So the top level becomes three lanes, colour-coded, and every subforum keeps its name one level down. Nothing is deleted, the tree is re-parented.

The three lanes need different rules, and that's the point. Trade needs identity and trust. Talk needs moderation. Tech needs archival search. One generic forum shell has been serving all three badly for twenty years.

06 · live html, not png

The Screens

Seven screens, drawn at both ends: the phone first, then the desktop that power users actually keep open all day. The mockups below are live HTML, not images, and the type inside them is a UI sans on purpose, so you can tell my portfolio's voice apart from the product's.

5.1 Forum index, desktop

The index stops being a directory of boards and becomes a feed of live conversation, with the board directory available as a filter rail. Three lanes across the top, one row per thread, and the right rail carries the two things regulars check anyway: what's moving, and what the mods just did.

1 · One line of live context. The last post's first clause beats "214 replies" for deciding whether to open a thread.
2 · The board tree becomes a personal rail. Subforums still exist, they just stop being the front door.
3 · The mod log is public and on the homepage. Enforcement seen daily reads as fairness, not surveillance.

5.2 Index & thread list, mobile

On the phone the same rows lose one meta line and gain a tap target. Lanes become a top pill row, sections become a filter sheet, and posting moves to a persistent bottom bar. The single biggest reason mobile lurkers don't reply is that replying is buried.

1 · "Jump to first unread" replaces page numbers. Nobody remembers they were on page 37. The system does.
Locked threads stay visible with the reason and an appeal link attached, instead of vanishing.
"Ask this section" sits at the bottom of the list, the moment intent is highest, not hidden in a header.

5.3 Thread view & reply

The hardest screen. A thread here can be 1,284 replies long and still contain three facts worth knowing. So the thread opens with a settled summary, community-written, mod-edited, versioned, and a health strip that says out loud what moderation has been doing. Replying happens in a sheet on top of the thread, never on another page.

1 · The summary is a wiki, not an AI verdict. Members write it, mods edit it, everyone can read the diff. Twenty years of goodwill says they'll do it well.
2 · Health strip over hidden enforcement. A removed post leaves a labelled gap with the reason and the mod's name.
3 · Redirect, don't block. The nudge offers a better place for the contribution, and "Post reply" is still right there.

5.4 Search that admits the archive exists

Twenty years of answers are unreachable because search returns posts, not conclusions. So results group by thread, surface the accepted answer first, and let you filter by the things people actually qualify on: lane, section, year, whether it was solved, whether the author is a verified seller.

5.5 Trade Zone: listing + seller

Trade is where the trust the community already has gets converted into money, and where it gets burnt. The listing separates the item from the person, and gives the person a record that took years to build and one bad deal to dent.

1 · Trust before price. The seller block sits above the fold with numbers that can't be faked in a week.
2 · Say the scam rate out loud. Hiding it protects nobody, publishing it makes the safe behaviour the obvious one.
3 · "Known for" is earned reputation, computed from where a member's posts were marked helpful, not a badge someone bought.
4 · Your own moderation record is public to you and summarised to others. Same rule for everyone, mods included.
07 · no downvotes

The Quality System Behind the Screens

Every quality feature above runs on one small model, deliberately without a downvote. Downvotes on a forum this old become factional weapons within a month. Instead, three signals: one positive, one evidentiary, one protective, and every one of them is attributable.

Helpful

Feeds "Known for" and sorts long threads. Weighted by the giver's own standing so ten new accounts can't manufacture an expert.

Source

Attaches a receipt to a claim: a price list, a spec sheet, a screenshot. Threads with sources rank higher in search.

Report

Goes to a queue with a visible SLA. The reporter sees the outcome. Bad-faith reporting costs you standing too.

Slow mode instead of the lock

A heated Kopitiam thread today has two states: open, or locked and dead. I'd add a middle one, slow mode, one post per member per minute, applied automatically when report rate spikes, announced in the thread with the reason. Most flare-ups burn out in twenty minutes. Locking a 1,200-reply thread to solve twenty minutes is a bad trade.

Veterans get tools, not immunity

Long-standing members with clean records can mark an accepted answer, edit the settled summary, and second a report. Real leverage over quality. What they don't get is a different rulebook. The status is visible, the powers are listed publicly, and both are revocable.

08 · what i don't know

What I'd Test Before Shipping Any of It

This is a concept study, so the honest part of it is the list of things I don't know. If someone handed me the real forum tomorrow, this is the order I'd find out in:

  1. Does the three-lane model survive contact with regulars? A card sort with 12 long-time members, half of them Trade Zone people. If Kopitiam refuses to be "Talk", the lane names are wrong, not the members.
  2. Does the settled summary get written, or rot? Ship it in three sections for a month. If fewer than one thread in ten gets a summary, the feature is a fantasy and the thread view goes back to plain.
  3. Does "Helpful" get gamed? Watch the weighting for a quarter before letting it feed search ranking.
  4. Does public moderation reduce appeals, or invite arguments? Measure appeal volume and mod burnout. This is the change most likely to backfire.
  5. Does density-by-default hold? Compact is the default here. If new members bounce, the toggle's default flips by account age instead of a global decision.
Takeaways
  • Legacy communities don't need less information, they need ranked information. I kept the density and changed the order.
  • The most valuable thing this forum owns is trust between strangers. Every screen I drew either shows that trust or protects it.
  • Moderation is a design surface, not an admin feature. Made visible, it becomes the community's evidence that the place is fair.
  • Redesigning something loved is mostly restraint. The temptation is to make it look like a modern app, the job is to make twenty years of knowledge easier to reach. Rombak, bukan roboh. 🤙
